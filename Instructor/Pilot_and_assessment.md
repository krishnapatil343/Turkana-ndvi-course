# Instructor pilot and assessment

1. Recover the thesis study boundary and site coordinates and confirm the rangeland buffer on satellite imagery. Record the source and coordinate order. Distribute geometry in the Student folder after verification. The present draft supports a student-drawn site.
2. Run the student script in an authenticated Earth Engine project. Check the NDVI mask, site, 96 monthly rows, missing observations and CSV column order. Save the export in this folder.
3. Paste the export into the workbook. Compare at least one raw and anomaly lag against manually aligned pairs. Save the completed workbook and example plots here.
4. Capture the Code Editor with site and buffer, Console charts, Tasks export and first spreadsheet pairing. Add these images to the Word guide with captions explaining what to check.
5. Time a first-time student. Budget roughly 1 hour for site selection, 1.5 hours for extraction, 2.5 hours for lag and anomalies and 1.5–2 hours for writing. Keep the interactive map and NASA sample optional.

Assess the site justification, units and screening, correct lag direction and pair counts, seasonal comparison, and interpretation. Accept different lag results where the site and evidence support them. NDVI is a greenness proxy, and correlation alone cannot establish forecasting skill.
